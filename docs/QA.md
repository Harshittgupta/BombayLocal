# BombayLocal: the 15 hardest questions

Asked by a skeptical **ZK engineer** (ZK) and a **Mumbai railway official** (Rail). Answers are two sentences each, and
honest. They link to [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) and the measured numbers in its §10.

| # | Who | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rail | **Can't one passenger share a screenshot of the QR with a friend?** | A code is valid only for its 10-minute window, and the same code shown again at the same scanner turns Amber, then Red, so a screenshot can't be reused there. Two checkers who see it within the same 10 minutes both accept it; uploaded scan batches only *measure* how often that happens (SPEC §8). |
| 2 | ZK | **What stops replaying an old QR or an old signed root?** | The scanner accepts only the current epoch (±120 s at the boundary), a root signed by an active issuer at or above its floor, a lifetime of exactly 36 h, and a root not yet expired. An old code fails the epoch check, and an old root fails expiry or the floor. |
| 3 | ZK | **Your Groth16 trusted setup has one contribution. Can you forge proofs?** | Phase 1 is the public Hermez Powers of Tau, but phase 2 has one demo-grade contribution, so whoever made it could in theory forge proofs. Production needs a multi-party ceremony, where one honest participant is enough; the verification key's hash is pinned in the scanner APK and checked on the audit page. |
| 4 | ZK | **Can a malicious issuer track users by giving each one a different root (tagging)?** | Phones accept only the issuer's latest root on Ethereum, at most one per on-chain interval, and the rebuilt tree must match it, so everyone converges on the same root. The residual: at low traffic, a buyer is briefly the only phone on the root published after their purchase, so an issuer colluding with a checker could link that buyer's scans until the next root (SPEC §8). |
| 5 | ZK | **Can scanners collude to link a passenger's scans?** | Only within one 10-minute window: the nullifier is `Poseidon(secret, epoch)`, so it changes every epoch, and without the secret, which never leaves the phone, nobody can link two epochs. The scanner keeps only the nullifier, epoch, count and read times, purged after about 20 minutes, and queues each first sighting (nullifier + epoch only) for at most 24 h until it uploads it to the issuer as statistics. |
| 6 | Rail | **How fast can you cancel a lost or refunded pass?** | The issuer publishes a revocation and a new root within about a minute, but a scanner still accepts the old root until it expires, up to 36 h. In an emergency the authority raises the issuer's floor on-chain, and every scanner rejects the old roots at its next hourly sync. |
| 7 | Rail | **What happens if Arkiv or Ethereum is down on a Monday morning?** | Nothing at the gate: scanning is fully offline, and scanners keep working for up to 48 h without a sync. Phones keep their last root, valid up to 36 h, and show "Couldn't update; your pass still works until …"; only new purchases and new roots wait. |
| 8 | Rail | **Doesn't proving drain the battery?** | One proof takes about 3.5 s on a mid-range phone (vivo Y100: 3,467 ms average), made in batches after unlock: about 45 s of work per 2 hours of codes. Showing the code costs nothing extra, and a check on the scanner takes about 50 ms. |
| 9 | ZK | **Why not just a signed QR ticket?** | A signed ticket carries the same ID at every scan, so it is a tracking handle, and a screenshot of it works forever. Ours reveals nothing stable, changes every 10 minutes, and a reused code is caught. |
| 10 | Rail | **Why not NFC cards like a metro?** | Cards need hardware for every passenger and gate, carry a fixed ID that can be tracked, and usually need a back-office check. BombayLocal runs on phones people already have, and the checker's phone works with no signal. |
| 11 | ZK | **Why use a blockchain at all?** | Privacy comes from the zero-knowledge proof, not the chain. Ethereum holds one 32-byte root per publish and Arkiv holds the public, expiring pass list, so anyone can check that the issuer is not editing, withholding or tagging data, and phones refresh without the issuer's server. |
| 12 | ZK | **Each phone downloads the whole leaf list. Does that scale?** | It downloads 32 bytes per live pass, so about 32 MB at a million live passes; expired passes drop off Arkiv on their own. That is fine for a demo but not for Mumbai scale, so production needs per-day delta sync and tree rotation (a tree holds 2^20 passes over its lifetime). |
| 13 | Rail | **What if someone steals a phone?** | On a locked phone the thief gets at most about 2 hours of already-made codes, because new proofs need the Keystore, which needs an unlock. The owner reports the loss and the issuer revokes the pass (see #6); the published release APKs are debug builds, so someone with the unlocked phone and USB debugging could inspect it. |
| 14 | Rail | **Checkers' phones have wrong clocks. Won't honest passengers get Red?** | Each sync measures the clock against the agreed Ethereum block time and corrects any drift over 60 s. A scanner off by more than 120 s between syncs shows a clear "Old code" reason, never a silent accept. |
| 15 | Rail | **What does one check cost?** | Zero: a check is offline and nobody sends a transaction. The only on-chain cost is the issuer's `postRoot`: 57,651 gas covering every pass sold since the last publish, so the cost does not grow with riders. |
